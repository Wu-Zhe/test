```Interview Process & Format – Zoom or phone screening, often with a small panel (example: 1 DA). For law clerk/legal intern roles, it leaned pretty straightforward, with a lot of “why this office” and interest matching. Question Focus – You’re tested on fit plus fundamentals, like legal terminology familiarity, your background, and (for D.A. tracks) fact pattern or hypo analysis plus an opening statement and ethics issues. Candidate Experience – Reviews describe a smooth, friendly flow with clear next steps. Background checks show up as the key “must pass” gate (example: fingerprinting) before you start.```

#### Interview questions collected
    - Why do you want to work for the office?
    - What law are you interested to pursue in future?
    - Why do you want to work for LADA?
    - Tell us about your background
    - What does justice mean to you? How has your background prepared you to be a DA? Why do you want to be a prosecutor?
    - What qualities do you possess that would make you good for the job?
    - Why do you want to work in the VIP Unit?
    - I was presented a map and asked where I would be willing to relocate.
    - Three district attorneys posed several hypothetical questions on criminal procedure law, including Brady, why you wanted to become a lawyer, and why you want to be a district attorney in particular
    - The initial interview process is very structured and time condensed. The first interview panel is with 2 senior district attorneys, after very brief introduction and a general question of why you want to be a district attorney, you are presented with 2 factual scenarios (not unlike a law exam) one dealing with criminal law and procedure and the other with legal ethics.
    - It was mainly the attorney talking about what my duties would be. I think that my resume was looked at thoroughly and as long as I didn't say anything dumb in the interview I would be hired as a legal intern. Be prepared to ask extensive questions to show interest.
    - The first step is an "exam," where you meet with two DDAs and are given a series of legal hypotheticals that you need to answer. Everyone interviewed gets the same questions. After that, they are scored. If you score high enough, you are brought in for a second interview that is more of a traditional job interview ("Why do you want to work here? "Tell us about..." etc.)
    - Why do you want to be a DA one day rather than a private lawyer?
    - Why do you want to become a prosecutor?
    - Why does your background make you an ideal candidate for the Deputy DA position.
  - 3 part interview First Part: hypothetical case analysis, not hard if you know basic crim pro/trial work second part: informational interview. Basic questions, they want to know if you are willing to go to any court location in LA County Third Part: Interview with DA
